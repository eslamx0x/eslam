ABOUT

Behind the Technology

I'm drawn to the space between how systems are supposed to work and how they actually behave under pressure. That gap — the misconfigured service, the overlooked input, the assumption an attacker won't check — is where most of my curiosity lives.

My work sits at the intersection of cybersecurity, programming, and open-source intelligence. I spend time learning how networks, applications, and protocols are built, then studying how they can be probed, misused, or hardened. I approach ethical hacking as a discipline of understanding rather than a shortcut to breaking things: reconnaissance, careful testing, and honest reporting matter more than any single exploit.

Outside of security testing itself, I write code to automate the tedious parts of research, experiment with digital-intelligence techniques, and study how attack surfaces form so that defenses can be designed around them — not bolted on afterward.

I'm early in this path and treat that as an asset: every system I study teaches me something I didn't know to look for.
